Host services: cancel and withdraw on stop; no late circuit, grant, child or timer after a ban

Root's probe (root-host-service-stop-race, transport de5bb98a) showed three problems:
- HostServiceTransport.close() returned while runtime.start() was still pending.
- The returned live circuit was then inserted into the closed transport, its exit grant was never revoked, and start()
  created its 15-s interval after the close.
- The privacy agent's judge() held `judging` across `await hostServices.start()`, so a known admin refusal was skipped
  until the startup returned.

WgCircuitRuntime.start({signal}):
- A stop ends the startup at its next step (err.cancelled). Waits that are pending (the tunnel's or the worker's
  readiness) are rejected at once, and their timeouts are cleared.
- A grant request already sent is awaited, never cut off, so a withdrawal is ordered after it.
- A host-service circuit that asked its exit and does not come up (cancelled or failed) withdraws that grant with a
  signed revoke through its still-running guard tunnel, before its processes end.
- If the revoke cannot be sent, the grant lapses at its expiry. The log says so.
- An explicit refusal (403) needs no revoke. App and control circuits are unchanged apart from the cancellation.

HostServiceTransport:
- close() is idempotent (one promise). It marks the transport closed, cancels the startups in flight, withdraws and
  closes the live circuits first, then awaits the cancelled round (bounded, 90 s).
- A circuit a runtime returns after the stop is withdrawn and closed (retire), never inserted.
- start() creates no timer once closed.
- A cancelled startup is no strike against the providers.

hostServiceLifecycle (used by privacy-agent.mjs in place of the inline judge):
- One transport at a time, following the platform host policy.
- A refusal stops the current transport, including one still in its first round, at once.
- Only an admit starts a FRESH transport, without waiting for it to come up.
- A late completion closes only its own transport, never the current one.
- close() resolves once every transport it stopped has closed.

The runtime itself and app circuits are untouched by a host-service stop. Administrator controls are unchanged: the
same gate, the same sticky refusal and the same explicit fresh admit.

Tests: test/host-service-lifecycle.test.mjs (9) on the real control flow:
- real WgCircuitRuntime (plain), WgGrants, two WgProvider instances behind their real control servers on loopback,
  and the real host-service ingress worker;
- the privacy agent's actual host-service block, sliced from privacy-agent.mjs;
- a stand-in wg process for the data plane only.
