# One recorded Moonlight stream to KVM Box, 2026-11-15 00:45-00:48Z

The owner's report: the stream "gets increasingly laggy after starting and will eventually freeze". This is one
stream of theirs, recorded at three places. Times UTC. Nothing was changed; no fix is built.

## The path

Moonlight on the workstation (960x600, 60 fps, 6000 kbit/s asked; the workstation and the NucBox share a LAN) →
the address `9a28642b.app.enclave.host` gave it → gateway on that edge (`network/wg-provider/gamestream.mjs`:
each of UDP 47998-48000 is one `enclave-port/1` tunnel, a TLS connection to the app's name) → the app's circuit →
`enclave-wg` on the NucBox → guest gateways → Sunshine (libx264, software).

The app's name resolves to the exits of the app's two circuits and to nothing else. At the time of the stream
(NucBox `network/state/status.json`, `apps[].circuits[]`):

| circuit | guard | exit | bytes up | segments | retransmitted | timeouts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | tor1 (ca-east) | fra1 (eu-central) | 86,477,728 | 123,018 | 1,098 (0.89%) | 34 |
| 2 | sfo3 (us-west) | sgp1 (ap-southeast) | 315,710 | 754 | 1 | 0 |

Counters are since the circuit was built (≈90% of circuit 1's bytes are this stream); of the 1,098, 463 were
slow-start retransmits after a timeout, 82 fast retransmits, 93 SACK recoveries. Moonlight used the eu-central
address, so every packet went workstation → Frankfurt → Toronto → NucBox, next to the workstation. Measured round
trip Moonlight → app: 0.36-0.41 s. From the NucBox the four providers are 23-34 ms (sfo3), 81-89 (tor1), 158-162
(fra1), 186-189 (sgp1) away.

Which provider is a circuit's guard and which its exit is not chosen by distance, and neither the owner nor the
host can set it: `selectNetworkCircuits` in `network/wg-manager.mjs` orders candidates by price, then by the
longest remaining qualification, then by id. With four providers at one price and two circuits per app, every
provider is used and the order of qualification decides the roles.

## Reading

At 60 frames a second Sunshine sends at least three packets a frame, ≈1.5 Mbit/s, also for a still picture. For
45 s from 00:46:12 the edge received 0.4-0.7 Mbit/s while the encoder kept producing; the 8.2 Mbit/s that followed
is more than the encoder can emit (4.15 Mbit/s plus parity), so it is queued video arriving late. At the end of
those 45 s the picture was at least ≈20 s behind (≥36 Mbit short at the 1.5 Mbit/s floor).

The video tunnel is one TCP connection inside the circuit, sent by `enclave-wg`'s userspace network stack
(`network/wg/tune.go`: cubic, send buffers 4 MiB by default and up to 32 MiB;
`docs/design/enclave-network.md`, "Circuit TCP": "a long, lossy path still limits one stream"). With ≈0.9% of
segments retransmitted and 34 retransmission timeouts on that path, one connection sustains somewhere between 0.5
and 5 Mbit/s and collapses after a timeout. A lossless tunnel does not drop late video; it queues it, in buffers that
hold many seconds of it. That is the lag that grows, and the freeze.

Not the cause in this session: the encoder, pairing, a circuit ending, the edge gateway.

## What could be done (none built)

- In Moonlight, by the owner: 30 FPS and about 2 Mbps. Halves the floor; lag can still build when the picture
  moves.
- Route choice: let an interactive app (or its host) prefer near providers, and name the nearer exit first.
  Roughly halves the delay and helps the TCP rate; with ≈1% loss the collapses remain.
- A datagram path: carry the stream's UDP inside the circuit as datagrams, so late video is lost (Sunshine's parity
  and key-frame requests are made for that) instead of queued. Changes `network/wg`, the edge gateway and the
  guest's gateway.
- Image only (`kvm-box/guest/sunshine.conf`): no parity over a lossless tunnel, a bitrate ceiling. Lowers the
  demand by about a third; cannot bound the lag, because the queue is on the host side of the guest.
