Conclusion
The useful financial invariant is narrower than zero post-checkpoint syscalls,
but stronger than successful JSON rename plus process death. Every financial
delegation capable of affecting the chain must be covered by either a reconciled
canonical prefix or a retained attempt in the verified final checkpoint/archive.
No relevant delegation outside that covered set may be introduced after capture.
Coverage and outcome are separate: a recorded no-hash attempt stays UNKNOWN
until reconciled; inclusion never authorizes replay, cancellation or replacement.

Define delegation as the first handoff of a transaction or signing/send request
to a component that can continue independently: transport, native worker, IPC
service, separate process, remote signer or RPC provider. The boundary is before
that handoff, not necessarily the final network write or sendTransaction return.
Already delegated bytes may be written or retransmitted after capture and after
sender death. Such traffic can be covered, but cannot be assumed covered merely
because it is asynchronous. If the signer/account has other writers, the scope
must include them or independently establish they cannot introduce new work.

Node22.23.1 semantics
Explicit process.exit emits exit listeners synchronously, then abandons queued
event-loop work. beforeExit is not emitted by explicit exit. Thus 'no await'
does not imply 'no application code executes after journal capture'. An exit
listener could synchronously write a pre-signed transaction or delegate it to
another process; scheduling an ordinary later timer alone would not work.
Primary documentation:
https://nodejs.org/download/release/v22.23.1/docs/api/process.html#event-exit
https://nodejs.org/download/release/v22.23.1/docs/api/process.html#event-beforeexit
This documents semantics; it does not establish the production loaded runtime.

Identified counterexamples and required evidence
1. Other signer users: direct wallet/writeContract/sendRawTransaction paths,
   alternate queues, imported/exported clients, another service holding the key,
   remote signer or pre-signed backlog. Inventory signer domains and all send
   entrypoints. One literal grep match in sessions.mjs is not whole-process proof.
   Production attestorEnv supplies no alternate queue, but generic APIs allow it.
2. Separate worker isolates, native addons, shared-memory queues or IPC recipients
   can introduce or finish delegated work while main JS is synchronous. Prove
   each delegation preceded its retained record or fence/reconcile that sender.
   A thread roster alone cannot establish what those threads can do.
3. Exit listeners and native shutdown hooks may run after journal capture. Review
   registrations including dynamic/preload/dependency paths. Earlier SIGTERM
   listeners may delegate before the JsonStore handler; their records must still
   be included. Runtime overrides of exit/emit/flush invalidate simple ordering.
4. JSON.stringify invokes getters/toJSON/proxies. Data/prototype mutations and
   patched JSON/fs methods can execute code while serializing. A hook after an
   array length is captured could append an attempt omitted from those bytes.
   Stores flushed after the journal can also invoke hooks and create new work.
   Establish plain data and reviewed hooks, not just absence of explicit await.
5. Multiple store instances/module URLs/reinitialization/other processes writing
   the same filename or common .tmp can replace a correct snapshot with stale
   bytes. Prove unique writer, actual store/path/inode identity, final ordering,
   successful complete write/rename, absence of later overwrites, and durable
   private readback after writer death. Catch-and-exit0 cannot prove any of this.
6. Retention500 can remove unresolved attempts. Reconcile a trustworthy earlier
   prefix and prove every later delegation is still represented across all
   checkpoints/archives. Same-nonce replacements are one outcome group, but
   retries, lost responses and different business operations must not collapse
   into invented identities. Counts and monotonic nonce alone are insufficient.
7. Serialization/write/rename failure, SIGKILL/OOM, wrong target, observer loss,
   incomplete thread coverage and no fsync can produce missing/stale evidence.
   A later fsync preserves observed bytes, not their correctness or freshness.
8. Disk hashes, current executable on disk and a sanitized synthetic fixture do
   not prove old PID loaded those bytes. Need justified startup provenance for
   Node, source/dependency closure, loader/preloads/options/native addons, and
   post-start mutation model. Do not obtain this by dumping keys/heap/environment
   into reports. No such complete production provenance is established here.
9. Termination at nonceN is a prefix anchor; earlier caught errors need outcome
   checks. Ended original SID does not fence other opens, owner ops or attestations.
   latest=pending, a bounded quiet period and no observed network writes do not
   disprove already-held RPC transactions. Canonical receipts/state and exact
   refund/accounting remain required before enabling any new writes.

This is a set of identified proof obligations, not an exhaustive claim about
arbitrary compromised runtimes. Some can be discharged with pinned code and a
trusted startup model; others require qualified observation and reconciliation.
Until their assumptions are evidenced, the narrower invariant is a proposal,
not a way to waive unknown activity in the existing trace.
