Two different latent-work cases:
  A. A preflight/queued Promise still inside old process: once ALL old writers
     are verified dead it cannot newly execute. Its unbroadcast work was
     abandoned, not drained; requests may need truthful failure reporting.
  B. A transaction already delivered to an RPC provider: it can be accepted or
     mined after process death, even without a disk hash. It stays an unknown
     financial outcome until identified/reconciled. Keep its nonce quarantined.
Exact OLD implementation ordering (sessions.mjs SHA457e8647...): _send first
awaits gas/fee/nonce reads, then pushes a "sending" record ONLY into memory,
then awaits wc.sendTransaction. On the normal transaction path the next flush
occurs after a returned hash or the generic send-error catch; there is NO
pre-send flush. HOWEVER, a successful synchronous SIGTERM JsonStore checkpoint
also flushes this in-memory sending record even while sendTransaction awaits.
While that synchronous handler runs, this process cannot resume async queue
work before process.exit(0). Thus a VERIFIED successful checkpoint plus writer
death can cover this process's in-memory send records; pre-record preflights
cannot later resume and send. It does not cancel an already delivered RPC call.
Any no-hash sending record remains ambiguous and needs nonce reconciliation.
If checkpoint success/identity is not established, or termination used SIGKILL,
an RPC-held transaction may be absent from disk. "All persisted entries mined"
is NOT by itself complete tail coverage, even with matching pre/post hashes.
The successor's new pre-send flush is distinct from the old shutdown hook.
Provider latest=pending equality and stable journal do not prove B absent.
Canonical termination fences original SID spending, but not an older signed
open, unrelated owner operation or attestation that was still in preflight.
