Enclave architecture: the app package, protection levels per host, verification, and status

This is the long version of the front page. It names mechanisms, says what each one protects against and from whom, and marks every capability as available, experimental, or planned. Where a result comes from one lab machine, it says so. Current as of 2026-09-23.

One app package available

An Enclave app is a WebAssembly component targeting wasi:http. Its bytes live on IPFS addressed by content hash, its listing lives in the EnclaveAppCatalog contract on Base, and the runtime inside the enclave refuses any bytes that do not hash to the CID in the deployment's on-chain record. The same component runs today on the confidential Linux fleet (compiled by wasmtime inside a confidential VM) and on the Windows host (interpreted by wasmtime's Pulley engine inside a virtualization-based enclave, because that enclave refuses every executable page a compiler would need). One build, no per-host binaries.

The domain contract experimental

Underneath the catalog, the backends are converging on one versioned agreement, enclave-domain-abi/1: a bundle is a canonical manifest plus the artifact, its identity is the hash of exactly those bytes, an attestation report binds the domain's transport key and nonce in its first half and the app's identity in its second, and every domain moves through the same starting, running, ending, ended lifecycle with reclamation exactly once. A Go reference implementation drives the Linux monitor; a Rust mirror drives the Windows partition launcher; both are checked against one set of conformance vectors. It runs in the lab on both operating systems. Nothing a deployment does today starts a domain through it yet.

What portable means here

The package is the same everywhere. The guarantees are not: they belong to the host. A bundle that runs on a confidential server and on a Windows PC gets the protection level of the machine it landed on, and that level is stated on the host's listing, in the attestation document, and on your dashboard. Portability is about your build, never about flattening the differences between hosts.

The limit we state on purpose

A confidential CPU defends memory against software: the host operating system, its administrator, and everything running beside the VM. It does not defend against someone with physical access and lab equipment on the memory bus, and current processors say so themselves. On the hosted fleet the operator is remote from the machines; a self-hosted seller owns theirs. That difference is real, and it is why a host's listing names who runs it and how it attests rather than printing one green word for every box.

Per-app hardware isolation inside the confidential VM experimental

On a confidential Linux server today, apps are kept apart by the Wasm sandbox and the operating system inside one VM. The research line below moves that boundary into hardware, one app per domain, on the same confidential CPUs. Everything here was measured on one AMD EPYC host in the lab, and nothing in the product starts a domain this way yet.

  1. M1 · M2One app per confidential guest, the app inside the launch measurementMeasured. The guest serves over TLS that terminates inside it, the key is bound into the signed report, and the report chains to the vendor's root under a caller-chosen firmware floor.
  2. M3aSeveral apps in one guest under a monitorMeasured. The monitor names each app in its report. The apps are separated by the guest kernel, which is weaker than hardware separation and is labeled that way wherever it appears.
  3. M3bA hardware privilege split between the monitor and the platform layerMeasured, with a limit. A measured firmware layer holds the most privileged level; the monitor and its apps run two levels below it and are refused the top level. The launch digest is derived independently and equals the live signed report. The digest covers that firmware layer, not the monitor image, so on this path the monitor's naming of apps is not authenticated by the report. That correction was measured on 2026-09-23 and is recorded in the plan, not papered over.
  4. M4aOne confidential guest per app, driven by the domain contract provisionalDemonstrated on 2026-09-23: 13 checks, two runs. Apps are separated by the guest boundary itself (own encryption key, own vCPU state), and because there is no monitor, the launch measurement is what names the app. An adversary with root in its own guest minted a signed report naming the other app and was rejected on the measurement. The proof chain is under independent review before we call this established; the cost is one guest per app.
  5. M4bOne hardware plane per app inside one guest plannedThe dense shape, and the one that mirrors the Windows enclave model. It needs the app-naming authority moved into the measured firmware layer. Hardware caps it at two or three apps per guest, so density above that always means one guest per app.

The plan, the negative tests written before the code, the correction, and the evidence scripts are in the repository's isolation directory.

Windows hosts

A Windows PC has no confidential CPU, so it can never be a host that cannot see your data, and nothing on this site says otherwise. What it can be is a host that keeps apps apart and states its level plainly.

The listed node available development tier

One consumer PC is listed today. Its apps run as interpreted Wasm inside a virtualization-based enclave; the host operating system cannot read the enclave's memory, but the machine's owner ultimately controls the machine, there is no RAM encryption, and app traffic is carried by the host side. The relay verified its TPM quote, measured-boot log and enclave report when it attached. Its build is test-signed, so its tier reads development, and deployments created before the node was listed are not placed on it unless their owner chooses it.

The partition-per-app backend experimental

The direction for Windows is one Hyper-V child partition per app, created through the Host Compute Service with no feature or boot change, booting the same guest image the Linux research path uses (byte-identical) on the kernel Microsoft already ships, loading the same app bundle under the same contract. It passed 30 checks in the lab on 2026-09-23. It does not exclude the host: the launcher signs the reports from the root partition, and every document it produces says hostExcluded:false. Excluding the host on this hardware would need a paravisor-backed isolated partition, which is the next boundary to probe, not a promise.

Shielded inference experimental

Most GPUs are not confidential hardware. Shielded inference is the research line for using such a card without trusting it: the confidential VM keeps the tokenizer, sampling, attention and every nonlinear step, and only the large matrix multiplies leave it, each activation hidden under a one-time pad before it goes and checked against a fast probabilistic proof when it comes back. A wrong or tampered result aborts before any output. The weights are public; the host learns shapes and timing, and can refuse to serve, and that is all.

Status, exactly: the engine exists and runs on one self-hosted confidential server beside its GPU, and the same protocol runs on the Windows node's integrated card. It is not on the hosted fleet, only chat-model decoding has been measured, and throughput depends so heavily on model, hardware and protection mode that we publish no number here. When a figure is stable enough to stand behind, it will come with the model, the hardware, the mode and the test conditions attached. Work on anchoring the trusted half in a phone's protected VM, and on offloading to a phone's accelerator, is research at an earlier stage still.

Independent infrastructure

The goal is a platform that does not stop working if this company, its servers, or its domain name go away. Hosts still supply hardware, connectivity, power and upkeep; the point is that no indispensable party sits between a deployer and a host. Part of that is built, part still runs on our machines, and the rest is a proposal. Each is listed under its own heading.

Already independent available

  • The catalog, the deployment ledger, the host registry and payment routing are contracts on a public chain. Funding a deployment is a transaction; a publisher's fee forwards to their wallet in the same one; nobody takes custody.
  • App bundles are content-addressed and hash-checked inside the enclave, so any mirror that serves the right bytes is as good as ours.
  • The source, the measurements and the release provenance are public, and the verifier runs on your side.
  • Confidential servers can be self-launched on hardware you own, from the same published images, with no vendor control plane in the path.
  • Your own apps on your own box run free: a zero-rate deployment needs no balance at all.
  • The CLI and the MCP server hold no keys. State changes come back as unsigned transactions for your wallet, or an agent's, to sign.

Still runs on our servers today

  • Discovery and placement at api.enclave.host, which also hubs the tunnels that make home machines behind carrier NAT reachable.
  • Traffic relays that splice app connections to enclaves by name and carry TCP, UDP and outbound IPv6. They never hold a TLS key and see ciphertext, but they are ours.
  • The site and the IPFS gateway. Availability only: the bytes are hash-checked in the enclave regardless of who served them.
  • The certificate service, which sees only a signing request; the private key stays in the enclave.
  • Card and stablecoin checkout, which creates and funds the on-chain record on your behalf.
  • The hosted fleet's container control plane, run by the confidential-container provider, and a published measurement allowlist that admits new hosts while the permissionless attach path matures.

Where this is going proposal

The candidate design removes the remaining indispensable parties one at a time: publisher-owned signatures over app bundles, a trust policy you keep locally instead of a list we publish, runtime measurements reproducible by anyone and verified on your side, several mirrors and peer transports for bundles, coordinators that are interchangeable or bypassed by talking to a host directly, and eventually secrets and state that recover without any party we run. These are proposals under discussion. The contracts and payment paths in use today are not being replaced by this page.
