Security boundaries & verification requirements+

Enclave Shield

Shield protects machines without confidential-computing hardware. Each app runs in its own domain, kept apart from the host OS by a smaller, measured trusted layer that the hardware's virtualization enforces. The app's identity is measured and its keys and traffic are bound to it. Checks fail closed, and a finished domain leaves nothing behind. Shield does not stop the machine's owner from inspecting data; only a confidential processor adds that. A Shield host is listed only after it passes the common acceptance tests.

Confidential hardware

Each app keeps its own domain, and the processor adds a boundary around the whole machine: memory is encrypted and integrity-protected against everything outside it, operator included. Before connecting, you check a report signed by the processor and chained to the silicon vendor.

GPU admission

A GPU in a supported confidential-computing mode runs inside the boundary. Any other GPU, even one beside a confidential processor, is used only through Shield's masked offload: the card sees masked inputs, its results are checked before use, and it never receives plaintext protected state. Admission reads proof of the card's own mode, never the CPU, the host, or a missing field. Until that proof checks out, the card is neither advertised nor routed.

Scope and limitations

Nothing here is called unable to see your data unless hardware enforces it and a signed report says so. A confidential processor defends memory against software, not against physical attacks with lab equipment, and a self-hosted seller has physical access to their own machine. A machine that meets only part of the contract is not listed, and ordinary virtualization with the host OS in charge is never built, listed, or sold. How billing works +

Rates come from the host. Your rate is your CPU and GPU shares at that host’s posted rates, plus any publisher fee, which goes to the app’s publisher. You see it before you fund.

Payments buy runtime. Prepaid time is metered by the second. Payments are final and unused time cannot be withdrawn, but it stays on the deployment’s public record so you can stop and resume later. Fund in small top-ups.

Resources follow your app. Each app sets minimum allocations, and GPU work needs an available GPU host. Shares are software-enforced allocations; they do not by themselves create hardware isolation. What does →

Rates round up. Per-second rates are rounded up to whole millionths of a dollar.
